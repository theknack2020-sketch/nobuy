Three directions
Five candidates were produced; two were eliminated (bottom strip). The survivors are presented in alphabetical order — no preference is encoded in position. The recommendation and each candidate's weakness are in the verdict, bottom right. Full measured contrast tables, beat specs, theme derivations and VoiceOver notes: docs/ROUND1_DIRECTIONS.md.
STRUCTURE, NOT SKIN — SHARED BY ALL THREE
· Four rooms only — Today, Calendar, Stats, Settings. The urge timer, impulse checklist and 24-hour waiting list enter from Today, never a fifth tab.
· The paywall is a door, not a room: it opens where a limit was met, states that limit in the user's own numbers, dismisses in plain sight, renders prices from StoreKit — no grid, no lock, no urgency.
· Five day-truths with fixed slots: no-spend, spent, mandatory-only, frozen, not-yet — every state carries a non-colour channel; nothing appears or disappears, a freeze unused is still a slot.
· The count is days, never dollars. One expressive beat per product — the moment today is answered, on the object itself, with a written Reduce Motion substitute.
· SF Pro with Dynamic Type to XXXL; targets ≥ 44pt; every contrast claim measured (WCAG relative luminance, computed — see docs).
· Five user themes = one token contract per direction; each direction names exactly which token rotates and which never move. Same content world throughout: run 23, best 41, July 19/6/3/1/2.
1aDoorframeTHEMATIC · DARK-PRIMARY
Point of view — the household tally: kept days are chalk strokes on the slate of a home you keep, and the wall keeps every mark ever made.
Signature move — THE GATE STROKE: the diagonal fifth stroke closing a bundle of five kept days. Encodes run length; second channel is geometry; recurs on Today, Calendar, Stats, paywall, both widgets.
Brave choice — dark is primary; the calendar is a wall of standing and lying marks, not chips; a spent day lies flat — a mark, never a hole.
Type — SF Pro only; Display Bold tabular count; the marks carry the character.
Palette — slate #1B1D1B · chalk #E8E5DA (13.4:1) · ochre #C98B4B (5.9:1) | plaster #ECE9E1 · graphite #33342F (10.3:1) · clay #8F5222 (5.1:1).
Depth + corners — none: the wall is one plane, hairline mortar only; radius 2-3pt stroke caps and buttons, 0 elsewhere.
Accent-swap test — five slates re-tint the wall; chalk and ochre never move. Meaning survives any tint because state lives in stroke geometry, not hue.

1bEscapementTHEMATIC-INSTRUMENT · LIGHT-PRIMARY
Point of view — waiting made mechanical: ten minutes, twenty-four hours and the closing day are one sweep on one silvered geometry; night is the same face read by lume.
Signature move — THE SWEEP: the arc of elapsed waiting. Encodes the elapsed fraction of a named wait; second channel is angle against ticks plus the numeric remainder; recurs on the day dial, urge timer, waiting bezels, calendar ring, circular widget.
Brave choice — the month is a dial of 31 ticks; the paywall draws its limit on the same face as the record.
Type — SF Pro only; Display Semibold tabular counts, Medium dial numerals; no mono, no serif.
Palette — silver #E8EAE7 · gunmetal #24272B (12.4:1) · blued steel #33567E (6.3:1) · copper #A65B41/#8F4A33 (4.1 mark / 5.4 text) | night #0F1113 · lume #D3D8A4 (12.7:1).
Depth + corners — flat faces, hairline ticks, one bezel line on dials; capsules for actions, circles for time, 12pt sheets.
Accent-swap test — five dial finishes re-tint the face; hands (blued by day, lume by night) and copper never move. Arcs and tick geometry keep meaning under any finish.

1cRuled OffNEUTRAL-PREMIUM INSTRUMENT · LIGHT-PRIMARY
Point of view — the account book as instrument: answering at night rules off the day's entry the way a bookkeeper settles a total; absence is the heaviest ink on the page.
Signature move — THE RULING: single hairline = open, double ink rule = settled. Encodes closure of the record (day, month, run); second channel is line count and geometry; recurs on Today, the month foot, Stats, the paywall standing, both widgets.
Brave choice — the hero is a blank ruled line, the absence itself; the good colour is ink — no green in the product; a text-only tab bar.
Type — SF Pro only; 100pt tabular figures over letterspaced small-caps chrome.
Palette — paper #F1F0EB · ink #1D1C18 (14.9:1) · oxblood #772F2A (8.3:1) · slate #6B6A60 (4.8:1) | night paper #141310 · #EAE7DD · #D2837B (6.4:1).
Depth + corners — zero shadows; hairline plus one value step of paper; radius 0 everywhere except the two answer buttons and price rows (4pt).
Accent-swap test — five inks (black, iron-gall blue, sepia, drafting green, violet-grey) re-tint the written truth; paper and oxblood never move. The ruling reads in any ink because weight, not hue, is the accent.

ELIMINATED BEFORE PRESENTATION (of five produced)
Mended Cloth — a woven record where a spent day is visibly darned, never hidden. Failed M-11: it would be the fourth craft register in a portfolio holding three (Coneglow, Saponora, Ovenspring), and its one great idea is an appearance, which may not cross to a winner.
Strata — days deposited as sediment bands. Failed twice: gravity-as-progress collides with Saponora's settle register, and compressing days into strata erases the calendar's first job — the shape the month makes.
VERDICT — WEAKNESSES FIRST, THEN THE RECOMMENDATION
1a Doorframe — the tally reads penal rather than domestic the moment the voice slips; the stroke calendar trades instant date lookup for the shape of the month; without texture (banned by the frame), chalk risks flattening to a generic dark theme in screenshots.
1b Escapement — nearest to genre convention: rings are the habit-app's native gesture, and Apple's own. The dial calendar is the weakest calendar of the three, and complication density pulls against one-decision-per-screen. The most beautiful, and the least inevitable.
1c Ruled Off — the coldest: its relief must come entirely from voice and paper warmth, and ink-as-the-good-colour can read on first open as having no colour system at all. Demands flawless copywriting forever.
Recommendation: 1c Ruled Off. It is the mission sentence made literal — closing a ledger you are glad to keep — and it solves the product's hardest visual problem most directly: the absence is rendered as the blank ruled line, the most substantial object on the screen. Its five-inks theme contract is the cleanest of the three, its translation to SwiftUI is the most mechanical (hairlines, type, two rules), and it is the furthest from every register this portfolio has already taken. Its coldness is real but priced in: the voice work is already written into the brief's requirements.

One graft (M-09, a measure, not an appearance): from Doorframe — the wall keeps every mark. Ruled-off history is never visually truncated or reset: ended runs remain in Stats as settled, double-ruled totals. A behavioural rule, no chalk crosses.
